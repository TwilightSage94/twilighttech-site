// FAQ

Things people actually ask

What can J-Bot do without asking us?
That is set per action. Low-risk reads can run automatically, routine changes can notify after completion, and sensitive actions can require confirmation or re-authentication. Prohibited actions stay prohibited. The point is an explicit operating policy rather than one global “AI access” switch.
Which tools can it connect to?
Common lanes include Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 plus ticketing, monitoring, CRM, and custom APIs. The audit identifies the smallest integration set that unlocks the useful workflows instead of connecting everything because it exists.
Where can J-Bot run?
On your Windows or Linux infrastructure, on one workstation for a solo operator, or in a Twilight Tech-managed environment. The permissions, budgets, and audit receipts remain explicit in every mode, and your custom configuration remains yours.
Will it replace a team member?
That is not the goal. J-Bot replaces repetitive coordination—copying state between systems, chasing follow-ups, compiling status, and preparing routine work—so people can spend more time on the judgment and client work they were hired to do.
How long until the savings are measurable?
A realistic window is four to eight weeks after launch. We baseline the chosen workflows before the build, tune during the first month, and compare time, volume, and exception rates after the system has real usage.
What happens if a model or provider fails?
J-Bot is designed for multiple providers and local models, with retries, circuit breakers, cost controls, and visible failure states. The exact fallback path depends on your deployment, but a provider outage should be an operational event—not a mystery.
Who owns the configuration after the Build?
You do. Your workflows, prompts, integration configuration, and custom business logic are delivered as part of the engagement. You can keep Twilight Tech for care or hand the system to another qualified engineer.

Which coordination loop should disappear first?

Bring the workflow your team complains about every week. We'll trace the systems, decisions, approvals, and exceptions—then decide whether it is worth building.
